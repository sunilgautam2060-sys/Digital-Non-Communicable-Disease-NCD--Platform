HEALTH CONDITION 01

Diabetes

Diabetes is a chronic condition that affects how the body regulates blood glucose (blood sugar). It occurs when the body does not produce enough insulin or cannot use insulin effectively.

Common Symptoms

  • Increased thirst and frequent urination.
  • Unusual tiredness or fatigue.
  • Increased hunger.
  • Blurred vision.
  • Unexplained weight changes.
  • Slow healing of wounds.

Common Risk Factors

  • Family history of diabetes.
  • Physical inactivity.
  • Unhealthy dietary patterns.
  • Excess body weight.
  • Increasing age and other individual health factors.

Prevention and Management

  • Maintain a balanced and varied diet.
  • Engage in regular physical activity.
  • Maintain a healthy body weight.
  • Attend recommended blood glucose screening.
  • Follow medical advice and prescribed treatment.

Important: Frequent thirst, urination, or unexplained weight changes should be discussed with a qualified healthcare professional.

HEALTH CONDITION 02

Hypertension (High Blood Pressure)

Hypertension is a condition in which the pressure of blood against artery walls remains consistently higher than recommended levels. It often has no noticeable symptoms.

Possible Symptoms

Many people with hypertension do not experience obvious symptoms. Very high blood pressure may sometimes be associated with headache, visual disturbance, chest pain, or other symptoms.

Common Risk Factors

  • High salt intake.
  • Physical inactivity.
  • Smoking and tobacco use.
  • Excessive alcohol consumption.
  • Family history and increasing age.
  • Unhealthy dietary patterns and excess body weight.

Prevention and Management

  • Reduce excess salt in meals.
  • Participate in regular physical activity.
  • Eat a balanced diet with vegetables and fruits.
  • Monitor blood pressure as recommended.
  • Take prescribed medicines as directed.
  • Attend regular healthcare appointments.

Important: Regular blood pressure checks are important because hypertension can exist without noticeable symptoms.

HEALTH CONDITION 03

Thyroid Disorders

The thyroid is a small, butterfly-shaped gland located in the front of the neck. It produces hormones that help regulate metabolism, energy use, and several body functions.

Common Types

  • Hypothyroidism: The thyroid gland does not produce enough thyroid hormones.
  • Hyperthyroidism: The thyroid gland produces too much thyroid hormone.

Possible Symptoms

  • Unusual tiredness or changes in energy.
  • Unexplained weight changes.
  • Changes in heart rate.
  • Feeling unusually cold or hot.
  • Changes in mood or sleep.
  • Changes in hair or skin.

Monitoring and Management

  • Consult a healthcare professional for assessment.
  • Complete thyroid tests when recommended.
  • Follow prescribed treatment.
  • Attend follow-up appointments.
  • Do not start or stop thyroid medicines without medical advice.

Important: Thyroid symptoms can overlap with other conditions. Proper medical assessment and testing are needed.
